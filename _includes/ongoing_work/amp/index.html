Alongside my exploration of CPG-based locomotion, I also became interested in a complementary question: how to make learned motion not only effective, but also more natural in style. This led me to study Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) [1, 2], which uses reference motion clips as an adversarial signal to encourage the policy to match the style of natural motion without manually designing complex style rewards.

This initial experiment gave me hands-on experience with the AMP training pipeline and motion-prior formulation in practice. It also strengthened my interest in using learned motion priors to improve the naturalness of robot locomotion.
